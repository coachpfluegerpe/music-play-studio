Music Play Studio

Listen • explore • create together
Touch, point, eye gaze, or partner-assisted choice. A seated response works for every activity.

Make it your session

Choose activities to fit each student's preferences and your session goals. These games offer practice opportunities; they do not assess or promise therapeutic outcomes.

Access: Use two choices for a simpler field. Students can point, look, use AAC, or direct a partner to touch. All activities can be done seated. Keep visual listening clues on for sound matching support.

Sound: Start at low volume. Enable sound with a touch on the board. These are synthesized tones and percussion, rather than recordings of real instruments. Stop sound ends any sequence immediately.

Possible goals: shared attention, turn-taking, sound discrimination, sequencing, choice-making, expressive music-making, and following start/stop cues. The therapist selects appropriate goals and adaptations.

Group use: Invite a student to lead, take turns at the board, or have the group respond with voices, instruments, gestures, or movement. There is no score or time pressure.
